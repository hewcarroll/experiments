A passively cooled datacenter GPU relies on air flow to dissipate heat, which is why it requires air to be forced lengthwise through its heatsink. This airflow is crucial for effective heat dissipation, as it allows the GPU's internal components to cool down efficiently. The lengthwise airflow ensures that the heat generated by the GPU is evenly distributed and effectively removed from the GPU's surface, preventing overheating and maintaining optimal performance.

When fans blow onto the card's cover instead of through the heatsink, several issues can arise:

1. **Inefficient Cooling**: The cover of the GPU is designed to protect the internal components and prevent dust and debris from entering. Blowing fans directly onto the cover can cause the cover to become dirty and dusty, which can reduce its effectiveness in protecting the GPU's components. Additionally, the cover may become overheated, leading to potential damage to the GPU's internal components.

2. **Reduced Heat Dissipation**: The cover acts as a barrier to the airflow, reducing the efficiency of heat dissipation. Without proper airflow through the heatsink, the GPU may not be able to dissipate the heat generated by its components, leading to overheating and potential failure.

3. **Increased Risk of Damage**: Overheating can cause thermal stress on the GPU's components, leading to potential damage or even permanent failure. This risk is higher when fans are directed onto the cover, as it can cause the cover to overheat and potentially melt or deform.
