- Under sustained load the card reaches 79 °C in about 70 seconds and the driver walks the core clock down to its 405 MHz floor. Generation falls from 26.9 to 6.8 tok/s, prompt processing from 250 to 94 tok/s. The heatsink as cooled at the time sheds about 69 W at that temperature; the card draws about 185 W at full speed.
- Throttled, the card uses 40–50 % more energy per generated token (9.6–10.1 J against 6.7–7.0 J).
- NVIDIA driver R580 exposes no clock offsets on this card (an allowed range of 0 to 0 MHz), no clock locking, no target temperature and no lower power states. The power limit and application clocks work, but even their lowest settings sit above the throttled operating point, so they can't lift the ceiling.
- R580's Vulkan driver reports 4×8-bit integer dot products as *accelerated* on the P100, which has no DP4A instruction. Current llama.cpp builds trust that flag and take an int8 path that made prompts 10× slower here (185 → 18 tok/s) and generation 30 % slower. `GGML_VK_DISABLE_INTEGER_DOT_PRODUCT=1` avoids it. LM Studio's Linux Vulkan runtime 2.17.0 has none of those kernels compiled in, so it is unaffected.
- Engine settings that helped: n-gram speculative decoding (`--spec-type ngram-simple --spec-ngram-simple-size-n 4 --spec-ngram-simple-size-m 16`) raised generation on a quote-heavy extraction job from 27.5 to 34.8 tok/s with byte-identical output, and cost 2 % on free text. Four parallel slots gave 1.65× the combined throughput at the same power. The same speculation setting on upstream b11988 *halved* extraction speed.
